# Developmental Unfolding Research Roadmap

**Research question.** Can a small, shared program that acts on local cell states explain reproducible patterns of neural development, and can those patterns predict a measurable property of a mature circuit?

## Stage 1. Test the mechanism in simulation

The synthetic experiment starts with one uncommitted cell on a 2D lattice. A shared rule updates its internal regulatory state using nearby cell signals. Cells divide into vacant neighboring sites and can adopt one of two stable model states. They form and prune signed connections during growth. At the observation time, the circuit freezes before the first task cue. Growth snapshots, spatial measurements, and label-budget curves are available in the [development report](https://statsleelab.github.io/developmental-unfolding/development.html).

Use two comparisons to separate questions about development from questions about circuit function.

- **Developmental mechanism.** Disable neighbor signaling while keeping the realized growth geometry. Estimate how this changes the excess of same-type pairs at distances beyond direct signaling. The type-shuffle reference preserves birth cohort and boundary position.
- **Circuit function.** Compare the grown circuit with a directly constructed circuit on the same positions, with matched cell signs and wiring budget. Assess sample efficiency by varying how many examples the linear readout receives. The zero-recurrence control keeps the intrinsic leak and input sites.

On 32 evaluation seeds, the grown circuit supported above-chance cue-order discrimination. The intervals for the added spatial order from neighbor signaling and the learning benefit from growth-time wiring both include zero. Before calibration, establish a reproducible cell-state prediction linked to an atlas measurement and verify whether the associated wiring improves a functional endpoint against a matched control. The earlier pruning experiment provides a complementary reference on a fixed growth geometry. [Protocols and results](README.md)

## Stage 2. Calibrate and validate with developmental atlases

Use the authors’ processed single-cell RNA data and sample metadata from **Braun et al.**, an atlas covering 5–14 postconceptional weeks (PCW) with complementary spatial transcriptomics. Restrict the first analysis to atlas-annotated dorsal telencephalon or neocortex. Extend the simulator to model a single cortical lineage with precursor, intermediate, and neuron-like states, and define correspondences with radial glia, intermediate progenitors, and excitatory neurons before fitting. The current two signed model states serve the synthetic circuit experiment. [Braun et al., Science (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adf1226) · [Processed data and metadata](https://github.com/linnarsson-lab/developing-human-brain#data)

1. **Design the split from metadata.** Tabulate donor, PCW, region, library, assay, and batch. Group all samples from a donor into the same fold. If the target region has enough donors across age and batch, reserve the latest represented age bin as a separate extrapolation test and use donor-grouped validation on earlier ages. If age and batch cannot be separated, validate across donors within shared age and batch strata and label age extrapolation untestable.
2. **Fit a limited set of parameters.** Use donor-by-region cell-state fractions to fit differentiation thresholds or transition rates and a shared monotone mapping from simulator steps to PCW. Weight each donor equally and use the same cell-state mapping across splits. Keep the growth and wiring modules fixed for this analysis. Filter, choose parameters, and tune the model using the training donors only.
3. **Score predictions with matched baselines.** On held-out donors, report mean absolute error in cell-state fractions and donor-level uncertainty against a pooled composition and a smooth age-only composition model. Check predicted spatial ordering on reserved sections from separate donors where comparable anatomical coverage exists. Use the independent cortical atlas of Bhaduri et al. to check qualitative marker and regional ordering across shared annotations. Compare numerical fractions across studies only where age, area, and sampling protocol support the comparison. [Bhaduri et al., Nature (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03910-8)

Atlas profiles are cross-sectional measurements. Pseudotime gives a relative ordering that can vary by region and lineage. Use donor age to anchor simulation time and report uncertainty in that mapping. Tissue sampling, dissociation, and sequencing can bias observed cell fractions. Cell counts improve within-sample precision, while donors remain the unit of inference. Multiple local rules can fit the same profiles, so report parameter sensitivity and which predictions distinguish rule families. Transcriptomic and spatial agreement can validate predicted cell states and organization. Circuit wiring and memory require separate functional evidence.

An optional later step can use paired expression and chromatin accessibility to prioritize candidate regulatory modules. Reserve sequence-level modeling for a defined variant → regulatory activity → cell-state phenotype hypothesis with evidence at each link. The immediate plan uses abstract regulatory states and processed atlas measurements. [Trevino et al., Cell (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2021.07.039)
